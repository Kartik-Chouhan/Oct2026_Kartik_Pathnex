CAB: CAB is the short form of "Change Advisory Board". It is a group of stakeholders responsible for reviewing, assessing, and advising on 
changes, especially production changes. helps evaluate the risk, impact, business justification, and implementation plan before a change 
is approved.

This board will finalize and will approve the production changes. Without the approval of board, not a single change can be performed in 
real time development practices. Change refers to the modification whether it is a fix or a new implementation in the production. 

What if we do any change in the production directly without the approval of CAB, then it can stop & bring the application down in the real 
time environment(production). It will charge as the penalty if the service of client or customer will be stopped or down. Also, it will 
breach the SLA(Service level agreement). 

The members of CAB includes Change Manager, App owner, Development lead, DevOps release manager, Infr or cloud team, Security team, DBA,
Business Owner.

Now, to perform any change in the production, we've to raise a CR(change request) that will be approved by CAB first. It is generally a 
JIRA ticket, which contains the details like change description, change title, assignees, reportes, approvers etc. It can include around 
25-30 people. Each JIRA ticket will be having a number to uniquely identify the JIRA ticket. Each ticket will be having the status 
as below:

1. To be Planned
2. In progress
3. Waiting for the CAB approval
4. Done (Completed)

We can add internal note(comments) in the JIRA ticket to give any update on the ticket itself. This CR ticket has priorities like P1, P2, P3, P4 and P5 based on
seveirity level of the impact on application in the production. 

P1 -- critical
P2 -- high
P3 -- medium
P4 -- low
P5 -- plan(to be executed in future)

Flow of CAB approval: 

---> Planning --> Awaiting Manager Approval --> Awaiting CS(Customer service) approval --> Awaiting CAB approval --> awaiting implementation --> Done 

Now, we should have a rollback plan in case if service will go down after performing the changes. It is usually the previous version of the 
running application. Also, there are two imp terms related to CR: -

1. Hot Freeze: During a Hot Freeze, regular deployments and planned changes are restricted, but emergency or critical changes may still be 
			   permitted with special approval. 

2. Cold Freeze: During a Cold Freeze, almost all production changes are stopped, including most planned deployments and routine maintenance.
				Only exceptional, business-critical or emergency changes may be considered under strict authorization.
